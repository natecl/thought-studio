# Product context

- **Status:** Founding context
- **Last updated:** 2026-08-28

## Founder intent

Bring whiteboarding back as a primary mode of thinking in the agentified age.

The product should do for whiteboarding what Notion did for notes: make a fundamental behavior beautiful and useful, then give it a small set of composable primitives that can support many kinds of work.

Thought Studio should be one connected workspace where a person can think, understand, design, architect, and create—alone, with other people, with AI agents, or with both.

## Problem

Learning, searching, communication, and work are fragmented across different applications and interaction models. Context does not travel cleanly between them.

A person may search in a browser, watch a video, write notes elsewhere, discuss an idea in chat, ask an AI assistant for help, and create an output in another application. The relationships between those actions disappear. Collaborators receive conclusions without the thought process. Agents return outputs without exposing how shared understanding was constructed.

This produces four losses:

1. **Context loss:** information must be repeatedly reconstructed.
2. **Spatial loss:** ideas no longer have a memorable place relative to one another.
3. **Process loss:** the path from uncertainty to understanding is hidden.
4. **Agency loss:** finished AI output can replace the user's active construction of thought.

## Foundational insight

AI made generation abundant while making thinking increasingly invisible.

Physical whiteboarding externalizes thought. It allows people to begin without a schema, add fragments quickly, arrange ideas spatially, construct systems gradually, and invite others into the same evolving model.

The opportunity is not merely to reproduce a physical board on a screen. It is to preserve those qualities while adding capabilities that physical boards cannot offer: durable memory, multimedia sources, provenance, search, collaboration across time, and visible agent participation.

## Product definition

Thought Studio is an agent-native digital whiteboard where people, information, and AI agents think together in one shared spatial workspace.

The core progression is:

`nothing -> fragments -> connections -> systems -> creations`

## Why the whiteboard is central

The whiteboard is not a canvas attached to a document product, an illustration layer for a chat product, or a diagramming mode inside a project-management product. It is the primary environment in which thinking and collaboration occur.

Other tools enter the board as material:

- Notion pages and documents
- YouTube videos
- Webpages
- Images
- PDFs and files
- Conversations
- Data and code artifacts
- Other boards

Integrations should strengthen the board rather than pull the user into separate mini-products.

## Audience hypotheses

The broad vision serves anyone doing complex thinking. The initial audience must be narrower.

### Primary hypothesis

Individuals and small teams who must understand before they create:

- Founders and product strategists
- Designers and systems architects
- Researchers and consultants
- Writers, educators, and research-driven creators

### Shared characteristics

- Their work begins ambiguously.
- They gather material from many sources.
- They discover structure during the work rather than before it.
- They need to explain or create something at the end.
- They collaborate with people or agents during the process.

## Jobs to be done

- When I have an idea I cannot yet articulate, give me a place to develop it without requiring premature structure.
- When information is scattered, let me bring it into one spatial context and see how it relates.
- When I collaborate, let others enter the model rather than merely read my conclusion.
- When I use agents, let me see, guide, and trust their contributions.
- When understanding emerges, help me turn it into a system or creation without losing its lineage.

## Desired outcomes

- People feel more capable of thinking, not merely faster at producing.
- Complex ideas become visible and discussable.
- Human and agent collaboration feels legible and controllable.
- Finished work stays connected to the evidence and reasoning that produced it.
- The product feels calm, tactile, expressive, and coherent.

## Product boundary

Thought Studio may eventually support many workflows, but it should not begin as an all-purpose productivity suite.

The first product is not:

- A general project-management system
- A conventional notes database
- A chat application with a canvas attached
- A template-first diagramming application
- An autonomous agent dashboard
- A replacement for every source application

## Open questions

- Which initial audience experiences the strongest pain and willingness to adopt a new thinking environment?
- What is the first creation type: product concept, essay, presentation, system design, or a flexible frame?
- Should the first interaction prototype prioritize pointer and keyboard, pen and touch, or both?
- How large should a board feel before navigation becomes cognitively expensive?
- What level of agent autonomy feels useful without violating spatial authorship?
- Which integration proves the value of connected source material first?
- How should private, shared, and agent-readable board regions work?
