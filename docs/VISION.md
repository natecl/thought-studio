# Vision

## Bring whiteboarding back

Whiteboarding begins with nothing. That nothing matters.

It gives people permission to place down an incomplete thought, move it, combine it with another thought, erase it, draw around it, and slowly discover a system that did not exist at the beginning.

In the agentified age, much of that construction has disappeared into chat streams and hidden execution. People provide instructions and receive outputs, but the evolving shared model is missing. Thought Studio restores that model as a place humans and agents can inhabit together.

## Product thesis

> Thought Studio is the shared whiteboard for humans and AI agents—a place to think freely, connect anything, build systems, and turn understanding into creation.

The board is the product. Chat, documents, sources, integrations, and agents are participants or materials within it.

## The Notion analogy

Notion treated notes and documents as a composable system rather than a fixed page editor. Thought Studio applies a related philosophy to spatial thinking.

| Notion primitive | Thought Studio analogue |
| --- | --- |
| Page | Board |
| Block | Mark, object, or source |
| Database | Connected system |
| View | Frame or lens |
| Human collaborator | Human or agent participant |
| Document output | Creation with visible lineage |

The analogy guides the level of composability, not the visual design or feature set.

## The future experience

A person enters a quiet, blank spatial environment and begins immediately. They write with a pen, type a question, sketch a relationship, or paste a source.

The board accepts almost anything without forcing it into a predetermined workflow. A YouTube video can sit beside a handwritten observation. A Notion document can sit inside a product system. A collaborator can draw a counterargument. An agent can research a bounded question and place findings in an assigned region.

Anything that can be represented digitally should be able to arrive as material with a legible origin rather than requiring a new product center. Over time, the same permission-aware Board and Frame context should be available to people, native agents, and external agents through an API, so every participant works from one shared model instead of copied context packets.

Every participant works on the same visible model. Agent activity has a cursor, scope, history, and identity. Sources retain provenance. Connections can remain informal or become semantic when useful.

Over time, fragments become clusters. Clusters become relationships. Relationships become systems. A frame around part of the board can become a product specification, presentation, essay, lesson, architecture, or other creation. That creation remains connected to the board that produced it.

## Human promise

Thought Studio should make people feel that AI expands their ability to think rather than replacing their participation in thought.

The product should protect:

- Curiosity before certainty
- Exploration before categorization
- Personal and collective agency
- The ability to understand why something exists
- The role of taste and judgment
- The pleasure of creating a coherent system from ambiguity

## Beauty and utility

Beauty is not surface decoration. It is the clarity of the spatial model, the feel of ink, the rhythm of movement, the restraint of the interface, the legibility of collaboration, and the satisfaction of seeing a system emerge.

Utility is not feature count. It is the ability to gather context, develop understanding, collaborate, direct agents, and produce useful work without breaking the continuity of thought.

Both are required.

## North-star behavior

The signature session follows this progression:

1. Begin with a genuinely blank board.
2. Add rough thoughts and external material without setup.
3. Arrange and connect material spatially.
4. Invite a person or agent into the shared context.
5. Allow a system or point of view to emerge.
6. Turn a bounded region into a deliberate creation.
7. Preserve the path from creation back to its thinking and sources.
