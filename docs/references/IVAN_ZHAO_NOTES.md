# Ivan Zhao interview notes

- **Source:** [Meet The Photographer Who Built a $10B Startup (Ivan Zhao)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GkEhuPCmAtU&t=488s)
- **Channel:** Sachin and Adam
- **Reviewed:** 2026-08-28
- **Note:** These are paraphrased product principles, not a transcript.

## Relevant themes

### Beauty and utility

Zhao describes beauty and utility as two necessary ends of product creation. The motivating ambition is not aesthetic polish alone or commercial usefulness alone, but tools that add both usefulness and beauty to the world.

**Application to Thought Studio:** The feel of ink, space, motion, collaboration, and emerging structure are functional parts of the product. They should be held to the same standard as integrations and agent capability.

### Art and business correct one another

Zhao reflects that Notion spent early years too far toward personal artistic conviction without sufficient market fit, then later experimented with rigid point solutions that conflicted with the product's modular character.

**Application:** Thought Studio must validate a concrete user outcome while refusing features that feel foreign to the board's primitives. Beauty without adoption is insufficient; utility that causes product-level “organ rejection” is also insufficient.

### Build with Lego-like primitives

Notion's coherence comes from modular pieces that can be combined across workflows. Large, hard-coded point solutions can conflict with the community, codebase, and builders attracted to the system.

**Application:** Build Thought Studio from a small vocabulary—Boards, Marks, Objects, Sources, Connections, Frames, Participants, Lenses, and Creations—rather than separate products for learning, research, strategy, design, and planning.

### Look beyond technology's immediate culture

Around the linked timestamp, Zhao argues that technology often behaves like a tinkering culture that copies nearby ideas while neglecting history, art, nature, and the wider humanities.

**Application:** Study physical studios, architectural practice, classrooms, research walls, design critiques, maps, memory, drawing, and collaborative workshops—not only current canvas and AI products.

### Context and tools make agents useful

Zhao argues that capable agents depend on access to relevant context and tools. Notion's accumulated knowledge and modular operations provide both.

**Application:** The Board should become shared context that both people and agents can inspect. Board primitives become agent tools. The visual model and machine-readable context should remain aligned.

### Human supervision and flexible interface remain important

Even as agents do more work, people continue judging quality and guiding outcomes. A flexible interface is needed for this supervision.

**Application:** Agents should work visibly on a flexible spatial surface with bounded scopes, proposals, attribution, and reversibility.

### Taste and agency become more valuable

Zhao expects execution capability to become more widely available while taste, judgment, drive, and willingness to try remain important or become more important.

**Application:** Thought Studio should amplify the user's taste and agency. It should not collapse creation into accepting an agent's first finished answer.

### Journey and craft

Zhao describes company-building as a continuing craft and journey rather than a destination.

**Application:** The product should honor process, iteration, and the visible evolution of thought—not optimize solely for rapid finished output.

## Derived design test

For any major feature, ask:

1. Does it deepen the beauty or utility of whiteboarding?
2. Can it be expressed through the same composable primitives?
3. Does it preserve the user's taste and agency?
4. Does it improve the shared context or tools available to participants?
5. Does it make the process of thought more visible and craft-like?
